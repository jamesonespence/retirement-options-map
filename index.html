Version v1_36
Release notes

v1_36

  • Removed the toggles from the Assumptions pop-up. It is now read-only: your choices are listed as plain rows, and the sale-timing strip at the bottom compares the five timings without switching. Change settings with the controls on the main page. Results are unchanged. The budget file and workbook are v1_36 too.

v1_35

  • The Assumptions pop-up is redesigned. It opens on a verdict (result, net worth at 105, a one-line reason why, and a colour bar of the spending timeline), then three tabs: This scenario, What happens, Fixed for all.
  • This scenario shows your choices as tap-to-change chips (retire, Markham sale timing, After Markham, inheritance, arrival year); changing one also changes the page behind. Markham, cottage, inheritance and borrowing details fold into short rows. What happens lists the key dates in order.
  • A new strip at the bottom shows the same scenario at all five sale timings with the net worth change, tap to switch. The pop-up is now a bottom sheet you can swipe down to close, with plainer labels (Taxable savings, Forced RRSP withdrawals). Results are unchanged. The budget file and workbook are v1_35 too.

v1_34

  • New Markham sale-timing slider replaces the Sell / Keep buttons. Five stops: sell at retirement, by 3 years after retirement, by 5 years, by 10 years, or keep as long as possible.
  • The slider sets the LATEST sale date. If the money would run out sooner, Markham is sold earlier and the sale is marked early. Until the sale the mortgage is paid from the portfolio at about $58.8k a year; the balance left is repaid from the sale proceeds.
  • The two ends are unchanged: Sell at retirement and Keep as long as possible give exactly the same results as v1_33. Budget amounts are unchanged.

v1_33

  • Budget file and workbook: fixed the red / green highlighting so it displays correctly in Google Sheets. No amounts, results or colour rules changed.

v1_32

  • Snowbird budget updated: second-residence rent is $5,500 a month (was $4,500) and the one-time yearly travel lump sum is $4,000 (was $3,000). Snowbird now costs $159.1k then $109.3k a year (was $153.1k then $103.3k), about $6k more.
  • Every Snowbird scenario got a little worse. Cases with an inheritance still last for life, but net worth at year 50 [103] is $0.1M to $0.9M lower (for example sell Markham, retire now, $1M less: $1.7M, was $2.5M) and the point money would run out moves earlier (that case now year 73 [126], was year 94 [147]). Snowbird with no inheritance runs out 1 to 2 years sooner. Toronto rental results are unchanged.

v1_31

  • Budget file: removed the Step 1 and Step 2 prefixes from the section headings. No amounts changed and results are the same as v1_30.

v1_30

  • Every year reference now carries Arthi's age in square brackets, for example yr 10 [63]. It shows on the timeline axis, the results table, the milestone chips, the spending timeline (years 1 [56] to 7 [62]), the assumptions and the footnote. Brackets are Arthi's age, plain 'age' is still yours.
  • Results do not change from v1_29. The budget file and workbook are v1_30 too, with the same brackets on the Assumptions, Summary and Scenario Detail sheets.

v1_29

  • New spending timeline for the scenario you picked, at the top of the Assumptions drawer and in Key assumptions: the years of retirement (with calendar years), the full annual cost including the mortgage, and where you live in each period.
  • The Owning Markham lines now show full amounts, with no more 'plus mortgage': $207.6k then $157.7k a year while the mortgage is being paid, and $148.8k then $98.9k once it is paid off. Sell scenarios no longer show those lines. A building-blocks table sits under the timeline.
  • Excel: the same timeline at the top of Assumptions (it follows Scenario Detail), with the building blocks under it; the inputs moved down 24 rows. The budget file is v1_29 too. Results are unchanged from v1_28.

v1_28

  • Spending now comes from your Budget Scenarios workbook, year by year, instead of one flat $135k plus $20k of travel. Years 1 to 10 of retirement use the retired years 1-10 budget (extra travel included), then the year 11+ budget. While you own Markham the Keep Markham housing costs apply ($148.8k a year in years 1 to 10 and $98.9k after, plus the mortgage), and the mortgage is now your budget's $58.8k a year (was $65k).
  • New After Markham switch: from the year Markham sells you live in a Toronto rental ($178.6k then $128.8k a year) or as a Snowbird, with the cottage as home and 5 months a year away ($153.1k then $103.3k a year). If the cottage sells too, its costs ($16.4k a year) drop out and Snowbird becomes the Toronto rental.
  • What it does: with a Toronto rental, the sell-at-retirement cases do a little worse than before, because rent costs more than the old flat budget (with no inheritance they run out about a year sooner). The keep-Markham cases improve, because you own the house on a lower budget: the two $1M-less cases that used to run out in year 40 now last to year 49 and 52 (Snowbird: 60 and 65). Every Snowbird case is better or the same. Savings stay $25k a year until you retire. All results re-run.

v1_27

  • CPP and OAS are now $25k a year after tax for both of you (not grossed up). You get half of that ($12.5k) from year 10 when you turn 65, until Arthi turns 65 in year 12, then the full $25k. It was $40k before tax from year 10. All results re-run.

v1_26

  • Two pots. Your RRSP is taxed at 25% on withdrawal (was 20%), so the $135k budget is grossed up to about $180k, and the mortgage payment is grossed up too. The inheritance and sale money go into a separate after-tax pot that is spent first, is not grossed up, and pays 12.5% tax on its gains as they are withdrawn (book value resets when the money arrives). RRIF minimums apply from age 71. If the inheritance has not arrived, a shortfall is still borrowed from it and repaid when it does, including from sale proceeds. Net worth at 105 counts the RRSP after tax at 25%. The inheritance amounts shown on the page and in the breakdown are corrected to the 4% growth ($2.57M at yr 10, $3.50M at yr 20); v1_25 still showed the old 3% figures. All results re-run.

v1_25

  • Her investments now grow at 4% a year until the inheritance arrives, the same as your own portfolio (was 3%). The inheritance amounts are higher and timing matters much less. All results re-run.

v1_24

  • Property is no longer sold early just because the inheritance has not arrived yet. If money runs short before it arrives, the shortfall is borrowed from the inheritance (at 4% a year). Markham (Keep) and the cottage are sold only once the inheritance has arrived, or if none is coming. The year borrowing starts is shown on each scenario (teal ring) and in Key assumptions. All results re-run.

v1_23
