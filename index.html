Version v1_28
Release notes

v1_28

  • Spending now comes from your Budget Scenarios workbook, year by year, instead of one flat $135k plus $20k of travel. Years 1 to 10 of retirement use the retired years 1-10 budget (extra travel included), then the year 11+ budget. While you own Markham the Keep Markham housing costs apply ($148.8k a year in years 1 to 10 and $98.9k after, plus the mortgage), and the mortgage is now your budget's $58.8k a year (was $65k).
  • New After Markham switch: from the year Markham sells you live in a Toronto rental ($178.6k then $128.8k a year) or as a Snowbird, with the cottage as home and 5 months a year away ($153.1k then $103.3k a year). If the cottage sells too, its costs ($16.4k a year) drop out and Snowbird becomes the Toronto rental.
  • What it does: with a Toronto rental, the sell-at-retirement cases do a little worse than before, because rent costs more than the old flat budget (with no inheritance they run out about a year sooner). The keep-Markham cases improve, because you own the house on a lower budget: the two $1M-less cases that used to run out in year 40 now last to year 49 and 52 (Snowbird: 60 and 65). Every Snowbird case is better or the same. Savings stay $25k a year until you retire. All results re-run.

v1_27

  • CPP and OAS are now $25k a year after tax for both of you (not grossed up). You get half of that ($12.5k) from year 10 when you turn 65, until Arthi turns 65 in year 12, then the full $25k. It was $40k before tax from year 10. All results re-run.

v1_26

  • Two pots. Your RRSP is taxed at 25% on withdrawal (was 20%), so the $135k budget is grossed up to about $180k, and the mortgage payment is grossed up too. The inheritance and sale money go into a separate after-tax pot that is spent first, is not grossed up, and pays 12.5% tax on its gains as they are withdrawn (book value resets when the money arrives). RRIF minimums apply from age 71. If the inheritance has not arrived, a shortfall is still borrowed from it and repaid when it does, including from sale proceeds. Net worth at 105 counts the RRSP after tax at 25%. The inheritance amounts shown on the page and in the breakdown are corrected to the 4% growth ($2.57M at yr 10, $3.50M at yr 20); v1_25 still showed the old 3% figures. All results re-run.

v1_25

  • Her investments now grow at 4% a year until the inheritance arrives, the same as your own portfolio (was 3%). The inheritance amounts are higher and timing matters much less. All results re-run.

v1_24

  • Property is no longer sold early just because the inheritance has not arrived yet. If money runs short before it arrives, the shortfall is borrowed from the inheritance (at 4% a year). Markham (Keep) and the cottage are sold only once the inheritance has arrived, or if none is coming. The year borrowing starts is shown on each scenario (teal ring) and in Key assumptions. All results re-run.
